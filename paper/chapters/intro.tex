The decentralized administration of the internet is one of the basic enablers of the rapid growth of the Internet as a whole, thanks to the autonomous nature of different network systems.
Different Internet Service Providers (ISPs) play a critical role in the functionality of the Internet, as they connect their end customers (Internet users) to the Internet backhaul.
The power dynamic of this system inherently gives control over shaping consumption patterns to the ISPs: consolidation of power at the hands of a few ISPs gives them the unique ability to control, throttle, or monitor the flow of information, in addition to enabling oppressive practices such as censorship and surveillance of end users. 
Most recently, communication disruptions due to airstrikes on main fiber optic lines and radio sites, and the depletion of fuel for generators, have severely limited the information flow to the population of Gaza since the start of the war in October 2023, halting humanitarian coordination, and leaving the affected population entirely cut off from life-saving information and assistance (\cite{gaza-brief},~\cite{gaza-report}).
In addition to these physical disruptions, governmental control of the telecommunication infrastructure often leads to the repression of communities during times of political unrest (\cite{digital-repression-palestine},~\cite{internet-shutdown-human-rights}), severely limiting the possibility for social actors to dissent and organize themselves.
Together, the aforementioned practices have led to a growing interest in peer-to-peer (p2p) messaging platforms.
Most notably, the rise of p2p messaging platforms like Firechat~\cite{firechat}, Briar~\cite{briar}, and Amigo~\cite{amigo}, have proven that there is a growing shift in user interest patterns away from traditional Centralized Social Networks towards diverse p2p social networks.
Physical p2p networks have a limited communication radius, draw support from their underlying hardware and protocols, and utilize multi-hop communication.
There are a plethora of protocols to use for p2p communication, and we chose to focus on stable protocols that most devices support and the populace are most likely to use in a disaster scenario: virtually all smartphones support Bluetooth and smartphone usage is largely ubiquitous, even in refugee camps and disaster zones.
By focusing on low-power communication, device battery life can be conserved to allow for critical communication in extreme situations: we thus decided to use Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) for our p2p communication protocol, which allows users to exchange messages over short ranges.
To achieve a higher level of information decentralization, and resilience to topology changes, we decided to focus on unstructured p2p networks as they are more suitable for dynamic alternative communication methods often necessary to guarantee free speech in disaster zones, during protests, and for citizens living under oppressive regimes.
The research community has proposed many metrics to measure the centralization, sparsity, cost, and resilience of global ISPs, Content Delivery Networks (CDNs), Certificate Authorities (CAs), blockchains, and signals (\cite{dependence},~\cite{comms-cost},~\cite{sparsity}), algorithms for selecting the most appropriate communication mode in Bluetooth 5~\cite{adaptive-phy}, and different signal propagation path loss models~\cite{pathloss}.
Most recently, systems and protocols were proposed to enable secure p2p communication between smartphones~(\cite{amigo},~\cite{bitchat},~\cite{briar}), but none of those works, to our knowledge, evaluate the metrics that prove those systems are actually resilient and appropriate to use in extreme real-world situations.
In this research paper, we conduct a comprehensive representational experiment to collect empirical data on the stochastics of the BLE signal propagation, and use the collected real-world empirical evidence to parameterize simulations of unstructured p2p networks, and then measure their centralization~\cite{dependence}, sparsity~\cite{sparsity}, and theoretical channel capacity, to elicitate a claim about the resilience of local networks.\\

\textbf{Contributions.} To summarize, this paper makes the following contributions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We present results of a comprehensive experiment to collect empirical data on BLE signal propagation stochastics conducted across different distances between transmitter and receiver.
    \item We show how the collected empirical evidence could be used to parameterize simulations of message passing in unstructured p2p networks at scale based on Bluetooth Low Energy.
    \item To evaluate unstructured p2p networks, we capture and present throughput, latency, centralization, and sparsity metrics measured in network simulations.
    \item We show examples for design considerations for p2p overlays that outperform state-of-the-art overlay designs based on throughput and communication latency.
\end{itemize}
