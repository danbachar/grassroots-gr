Recent crises, natural disasters, and large-scale protests have shown that people heavily rely on online social networks and instant messaging platforms to communicate.
During crises, communication networks can be strained, partially or wholly destroyed, censored, or otherwise monitored, leaving reliant communities in need of alternatives.
An alternative to these centralized, concentrated, and consolidated networks is decentralized peer-to-peer overlay networks.
In this paper, we provide empirical insights to parametrize simulations of local area networks at scale based on Bluetooth. We then define resilience based on sparsity to capture the performance of the overlay.
We show design considerations for the overlay to outperform state-of-the-art overlay designs based on resilience and communication latency.
