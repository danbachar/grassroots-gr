The results shown at figure~\ref{fig:centralization-vs-max-deg} and in more depth at figure~\ref{fig:centralization-vs-max-deg-full} in the appendix, indicate that increasing the maximum constrained node degree generally increases the inequality and centralization of the resulting topology, as measured by both the Gini index and the Centralization Score~$\mathscr{S}$.
This effect is more pronounced in higher communication ranges, as visible with the stagnation in the $\mathscr{S}$-score for medium communication ranges 50-80m, around the maximum allowed node degree of 10.
The Gini coefficient exhibits less of a stagnation effect, which indicates it is more sensitive to changes in the topology than the $\mathscr{S}$-Score.
This is due to the fact that the Gini coefficient depends on the entire node degree distribution, whereas the $\mathscr{S}$-Score depends mostly on the top dominant nodes.
Because of the inherent focus of the $\mathscr{S}$-Score on the top dominant nodes, it saturates quickly as the maximum allowed node degree increases, as the topologies exhibit less of a star-like structure. It is however noteworthy to observe that both metrics are usable, albeit for different goals: the Gini coefficient is more appropriate to measure resilience as quantified by an even distribution of node degrees, while the $\mathscr{S}$-Score is more appropriate to detect single points of failure.
To design a topology that is more resilience, it is therefore important to consider both the intended commununication range, centralization rating, and the physical distribution of nodes: if the nodes are geographically dispersed and a higher communication range is needed, the established network might result in a sparsely-populated network which is subsceptible to centralization effects such as vulnerability to outages. 
Furthermore, in unstructured p2p networks, employing gossip to disseminate information without consideration of the peer degree, can lead to a very high message bloat that may lead to communication bottlenecks and reduced resilience, as nodes with a high peer degree become quickly overwhelmed with messages to process, and can no longer effectively communicate themselves.
Lastly, the cost of communication-intensive algorithms, such as ones used by epidemic routers or structured p2p networks that are both characterised by a high messaging overhead, should be considered when designing unstructured p2p networks, as they can quickly saturate the limited channel capacity of BLE links, leading to increased message latency.
