The results shown at figure~\ref{fig:centralization-vs-max-deg} and in more depth at figure~\ref{fig:centralization-vs-max-deg-full-high} in the appendix, indicate that increasing the maximum constrained node degree generally increases the inequality and centralization of the resulting topology, as measured by both the Gini index and the Centralization Score~$\mathscr{S}$.
This effect is more pronounced in higher communication ranges, as visible with the stagnation in the $\mathscr{S}$-score for medium communication ranges 50-80m, around the maximum allowed node degree of 10.
The Gini coefficient exhibits less of a stagnation effect, which indicates it is more sensitive to changes in the topology than the $\mathscr{S}$-Score.
This is due to the fact that the Gini coefficient depends on the entire node degree distribution, whereas the $\mathscr{S}$-Score depends on the number of links and the distribution of degrees, which leads to the increase in number of links cancelling out the increase in node degrees.
The Gini coefficient shows a more consistent and steady increase in correlation with an increase in the maximum allowed node degree.
To design a topology that is more resilience, it is therefore important to consider both the intended commununication range, and the physical distribution of nodes: if the nodes are geographically dispersed and a higher communication range is needed, the established network might result in a sparsely-populated network which is subsceptible to centralization effects such as vulnerability to outages.
